# AI traction ladders

Four dimensions, two ladders each. Every rung is an observable behaviour, not a rating.
Place the organisation on the rung that matches what is actually happening — from
artifacts where you have them, from what the reader describes where you do not.

Rungs are labelled **Theater / Mixed / Traction** rather than 1/3/5. The numbers exist
in the source scorecard to produce a score; here they would only invite arithmetic.

---

## Strategic Focus

*Are AI bets anchored in real, high-priority business bottlenecks?*

### How AI initiatives get started

- **Theater** — Based on new tech trends or what competitors are doing.
- **Mixed** — From general business goals, but the specific problem isn't always clear.
- **Traction** — Directly from a specific, well-understood business or customer bottleneck.

### What the kickoff conversation sounds like

- **Theater** — Almost entirely about the tech: models, vendors, and architecture.
- **Mixed** — We talk about the business problem, but quickly pivot to debating the tech solution.
- **Traction** — A disciplined focus on defining the job to be done before exploring any tech.

**Where to look for evidence:** the initiating doc or ticket for the two most recent AI
initiatives. Does the first paragraph name a bottleneck or a technology? Whose name is
on it — someone who owns the business problem, or someone who owns the platform?

## Execution Model

*Are you de-risking with small, fast experiments instead of big-bang roadmaps?*

### How you know an initiative is heading in the right direction

- **Theater** — We track the completion of tasks and the delivery of the project plan.
- **Mixed** — We look at proxy metrics, like user engagement, but we're not always sure how they connect to business impact.
- **Traction** — We define and measure leading indicators — changes in customer or user behaviour — that we have evidence to believe will drive the ultimate business outcome.

### What the roadmap looks like

- **Theater** — A detailed, feature-based plan with timelines and deliverables.
- **Mixed** — A list of features we want to build, but we're flexible on the timing.
- **Traction** — A series of target outcomes or problems to solve, giving teams the space to experiment and discover the best solutions.

**Where to look for evidence:** the actual roadmap artifact, and the last status update
sent to a sponsor. Count how many rows are named after a thing to build versus a number
to move. Ask what got stopped in the last two quarters, and why — an execution model
that never kills anything is not running experiments, it is running a plan.

## Team Integration

*Are AI capabilities embedded where the customer and business context lives?*

### What capabilities teams have to explore and leverage AI

- **Theater** — No real capability; they rely on central shared resources.
- **Mixed** — Specialised AI experts are circulated around and assigned to teams based on need.
- **Traction** — Teams have access to AI enablement (platforms, training) to develop their own capabilities and deliver value.

### Who is empowered to experiment

- **Theater** — Only a specialised AI team or senior leadership can approve and run experiments.
- **Mixed** — Product teams have the autonomy to run experiments within their own domains.
- **Traction** — A core competency: anyone close to a customer problem is encouraged and enabled to experiment.

**Where to look for evidence:** an org chart is weaker evidence than a queue. Is there a
request queue in front of the AI group? How long is it? Who has actually shipped an AI
change in the last quarter — count distinct teams, not distinct people.

## Pragmatic Mindset

*Is AI treated as leverage for known workflows, or as a solution in search of a problem?*

### The organisation's primary philosophy for using AI

- **Theater** — Building a grand, centralised AI "brain" or platform for the future.
- **Mixed** — A mix of big-picture projects and practical workflow improvements.
- **Traction** — Applying AI pragmatically to accelerate specific, known workflows.

### The primary driver for selecting and prioritising AI work

- **Theater** — External signals: competitor moves, vendor hype, and tech trends.
- **Mixed** — A mix of our strategic goals and reacting to external trends.
- **Traction** — A disciplined process of solving the top identified business or customer constraint, or tapping a strategic opportunity by enabling new customer behaviours.

**Where to look for evidence:** the ratio of platform/enablement work to
workflow-in-production work on the current board. A platform with no workflow behind it
is the clearest single tell for this dimension.

## The three patterns

Name the pattern the evidence supports. Do not compute a score.

### AI Theater — high activity, low traction

The organisation is active with AI but probably not seeing measurable business outcomes.
This happens when projects are led by technology excitement rather than tied to a
validated customer or business constraint.

The shift needed is from large, speculative builds to a disciplined cadence of small,
fast, testable experiments. Start with one specific bottleneck and focus all energy on
proving you can move that number.

Raw material for the one experiment:
- Pick one costly constraint; write a one-sentence problem statement.
- Run a 1-2 week manual "Be the Bot" test with 3-5 users.
- Decide: double down, redesign, or drop, based on evidence. Don't chase sunk costs.

### Pragmatic Builder — solid foundation, inconsistent progress

Good instincts, real problems being solved, but progress is lumpy or hero-dependent.
There have been wins; they are hard to repeat. The missing piece is usually a consistent
operating rhythm for how AI work gets selected, tested, and shipped.

The goal is to make successes repeatable. Codify the rhythm: how is a constraint
selected? How is a manual validation run? What are the exit criteria for an experiment?
That is what turns individual wins into an organisational capability.

Raw material for the one experiment:
- Adopt a weekly AI experiment cadence with explicit learning goals.
- Instrument one leading indicator tied to the business constraint being solved.
- Embed AI skills inside the product teams closest to customers.

### Force Multiplier — AI as a true leverage play

Clarity, discipline, compounding wins. AI potential is translating into tangible business
advantage. The next challenge is scaling the capability without introducing bureaucracy:
codify the playbook, expand to adjacent constraints, and keep governance enabling speed
rather than stifling it.

Raw material for the one experiment:
- Standardise and share the patterns that worked (prompt libraries, data flows, review rituals).
- Consider a small, lean platform team supporting the embedded product teams.
- Publish short internal case studies of wins *and* failures to accelerate learning.

## Mixed readings

A single pattern across all four dimensions is rare and slightly suspicious. The common
and more useful reading is a split — most often Traction on Strategic Focus and Theater
on Team Integration, which is the signature of an organisation whose strategy is sound
and whose capability is bottlenecked behind a central group.

When dimensions disagree, the lowest one is the constraint. Recommend against it, not
against the average.
