# Product operating model probes

Forty probes across four dimensions. Each one is a question with observable answers —
the point is to place the organisation from evidence, not to collect forty ratings.

The **short set** (marked ★) is ten probes that reach a usable read in one sitting. Use
it by default. Go wide only when the short set comes back ambiguous, or when the reader
wants the full audit for a leadership team to work through together.

Rungs read **Project / Transitional / Product** rather than 1/3/5.

---

## Strategic Alignment

★ **How is success primarily defined for your top-priority initiatives?**
- Project — Shipping the defined scope on time and budget (output).
- Transitional — Hitting roadmap milestones and internal delivery dates.
- Product — Driving specific customer behaviour changes or business KPIs (outcome).

★ **When a strategic bet shows negative data early, what typically happens?**
- Project — We stick to the plan to avoid "failing."
- Product — We pivot or kill it.

★ **Are budgets tied to temporary projects or long-lived value streams?**
- Project — Annual project-based funding with business cases.
- Transitional — Quarterly departmental budgets.
- Product — Persistent funding for long-lived value streams.

**How often does funding shift between product lines on fresh market evidence?**
Annually (rigid) → Quarterly or continuous (dynamic).

★ **Do your OKRs look like a list of projects with a percentage sign at the end?**
Yes / No. If the reader uses OKRs at all, this is the fastest tell in the whole audit.

**In leadership meetings, what carries more weight** — the highest-paid person's opinion,
or objective evidence from the value cycle?

**Can every team member explain how their current task drives a top-level business
outcome?** Rarely, they just know the ticket → Always, alignment is crystal clear.

**Is value defined by what we built (output) or by a change in customer behaviour
(outcome)?**

**When starting a large initiative, do you build a tracer bullet (thin slice) first?**
Yes / No.

**Does your technology roadmap drive business strategy, or is it a to-do list handed down
by stakeholders?** Reactive to-do list → Strategic value driver.

## People and Leadership

★ **Do your product teams contain everyone needed to ship — design, QA, data, dev —
without hand-offs?**
- Project — Constant departmental hand-offs.
- Transitional — Some integration, but silos remain.
- Product — Fully integrated cross-functional pods.

**Are teams disbanded when a project ends, or do they stay together to own the product's
evolution?** Temporary/project-based → Long-lived/product-based.

**Do leaders focus on assigning the how, or defining the why?** Taskmaster → Steward.

**Can teams self-organise to solve problems, or must every move be approved by a
functional manager?** Locked by functional silos → Fluid skill liquidity.

**How often do teams challenge the feasibility or value of a requested feature in a
meeting?** Never (low safety) → Frequently (high safety).

**Are you dependent on specific individuals who are single points of failure for whole
products?** Yes / No.

**Do teams feel responsible for the business success of the product, or just technical
delivery?**

**Do your lead engineers spend more time on innovation or firefighting?**

**Is bad news shared early, or watermeloned — green outside, red inside — until the
deadline?** This one is worth asking twice, phrased differently.

**Are managers incentivised by their department's budget and headcount, or by
cross-functional outcomes?**

## Structure and Governance

★ **How many approvals or gates exist between a finished feature and production?**
- Project — 5+ manual approvals (security, finance, QA, CAB…).
- Transitional — 2-4 manual approvals.
- Product — Automated governance / team-owned release.

★ **How is a technical dependency between teams typically handled?**
- Project — Escalated to a PMO or manager to schedule.
- Product — Teams self-organise and resolve it directly.

**Is the organisation structured around technical components or customer-facing value
streams?**

**Can a product team spin up their own environment or tools without opening a ticket and
waiting?** Everything is a ticket → True self-service platform.

**What is the average decision latency for an executive decision that affects a team's
flow?** Weeks / Days / Hours.

**Does leadership focus on starting new things (left-to-right) or finishing current
things (right-to-left)?**

**Is there a single source of truth — a portfolio kanban — showing the status of all
strategic flow?** Yes / No.

**Does your PMO track status and compliance, or flow and value?**

**Are vendors managed by delivering scope, or delivering capacity and outcomes?**

**How many different teams must touch a single customer-facing feature before it reaches
GA?** 4+ (high friction) → 1-2 (low friction).

**Does your governance model feel like it protects the org *from* the teams, or enables
them?**

## The Value Cycle

★ **How often do you ship value to production?**
- Project — Twice a year or less (big-bang releases).
- Transitional — Monthly increments.
- Product — Daily or multiple times per week.

★ **How much discovery — customer research and validation — do your engineers
participate in?**
- Project — None; they receive "ready" requirements to build.
- Transitional — They see occasional research reports.
- Product — They join customer calls and participate in ideation.

★ **Does your process require a learning receipt — proof of value — before funding the
next slice?** Yes / No.

**Does product discovery happen as a daily activity or a phase at the start?**

**How quickly do you get actual usage data after a feature goes live?** Months / Weeks /
Real-time.

**Do you use feature flags to decouple deploying code from releasing value?** Yes / No.

**How often do developers talk directly to the people using the product?** Never
(mediated) → Weekly (direct).

**Does the team that builds the product also support it?** Yes / No.

**Are teams rewarded for learning that an idea was bad as much as for shipping a good
one?** Failure is penalised → Learning is celebrated.

**Is there dedicated capacity for technical debt, or is it always sacrificed for
features?**

**When a failure happens, is the reaction blame and process tightening, or a blame-free
post-mortem?**

## The three patterns

### The Project Silo — the agile is skin deep

A traditional project-management engine with updated meeting names. Expensive product
talent, strangled by bureaucratic hand-offs and fixed output roadmaps. The signature is
high decision latency and a traffic jam of work in progress: teams visibly busy, the
business strategy not moving.

The distinctive risk is expensive failure on big bets, because there are no
evidence-based loops to pivot early.

Raw material for the one experiment:
- Stop starting new things; visualise and clear the traffic jam first.
- Implement a portfolio kanban to bring transparency to leadership.
- Start requiring learning receipts — data — before funding the next phase.

### The Transitional Lab — the messy middle

The right principles are in place and the old operating system is still running behind
them. There are "product teams," but they are order-takers rather than mission-owners.
Outcome-based intentions, execution still slowed by cross-team dependencies and OKR
theater, where goals are tasks with a percentage sign.

This is where most organisations who take this audit land, and the failure mode is
declaring victory on the principles while the gravity pulls execution back.

Raw material for the one experiment:
- Unify discovery and delivery: get engineers into customer validation.
- Shift from project funding to persistent value-stream funding.
- Descale governance: replace manual approval gates with automated guardrails.

### Product-Led Engine — a high-traction value engine

Business and technology goals integrated, teams as empowered mission-owners, structure
descaled enough for daily value delivery. The remaining challenges are bet optimisation
and preventing complexity creep as it scales.

Raw material for the one experiment:
- Focus on talent density: keep the best people close to discovery work.
- Audit the operating model as a product itself, to prevent bureaucratic bloat.
- Invest in platform thinking so teams stay genuinely autonomous.

## Reading the dimensions against each other

The average is the least informative number here. What matters is the shape:

- **Strong Value Cycle, weak Structure & Governance** — the most common and most
  frustrating shape. Teams that could ship daily, gated to monthly. The constraint is
  approvals, not capability, and no amount of team-level coaching moves it.
- **Strong Strategic Alignment, weak People & Leadership** — a clear strategy nobody is
  empowered to act on. Usually shows up as high decision latency and watermelon reporting.
- **Everything mid** — genuine messy middle. Pick the dimension where one change
  unblocks the others; it is almost always governance.
- **Strong everywhere except one probe** — treat a single outlier as a measurement error
  until it is confirmed twice. One bad answer is not a pattern.
