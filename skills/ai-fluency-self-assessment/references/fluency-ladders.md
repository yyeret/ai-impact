# AI fluency ladders

Four dimensions. The rungs are observable behaviours — what someone does in a week, not
how capable they feel. Place the reader from what they describe doing and, where you can
see it, from the artifacts: their saved projects and custom instructions, the prompts
in their repo, whether anything is written down for anyone but them.

---

## The three modes of engagement

Three separate ladders, not three levels of one thing. Someone can be strong on
augmentation and absent on agency; that combination is the most common reading and it
has a specific fix.

### Automation — offloading a task with a fixed recipe

- **Manual / rarely** — Routine tasks are still done by hand; AI is occasional.
- **Occasional** — A few recurring tasks are offloaded when they come to mind.
- **Daily / high-volume** — Routine work with a known recipe is reliably handed off.

### Augmentation — AI as an active thinking partner

- **I think solo** — Thinking happens first; AI is asked to write up the conclusion.
- **Sometimes** — AI is consulted on hard problems, mostly to check work.
- **Integrated co-creation** — AI is used to challenge logic and surface blind spots as part of thinking, not after it.

### Agency — delegating an objective and letting the AI determine the how

- **I micromanage prompts** — Every step is specified; the AI executes a sequence.
- **Partial** — Some middle steps are delegated, with frequent check-ins.
- **I delegate objectives** — Goals are handed over; the AI determines the approach and manages the middle steps.

**The Agency Ceiling** is the most common blocker for capable users: fluent at
augmentation, still babysitting the tool through steps it could handle alone. It is not
a skill gap — it is a trust-and-verification gap, which means the fix is a verification
protocol, not more prompting practice.

## Context and instructions

### Instruction style

- **Zero description** — Short, one-line commands.
- **Detailed** — Tasks with persona and format specified.
- **Structural** — Role, context, constraints, and reasoning approach.

### How the AI knows the world you operate in — goals, brand, constraints

This is the highest-leverage ladder in the whole assessment and the one people skip.

- **Prompt-only** — Explained from scratch in every new chat.
- **Prompt-specific** — Files manually pasted or uploaded into particular sessions.
- **Persistent** — System instructions, projects, or custom agents carry the context.
- **Living** — The AI reaches live data through MCP, APIs, or connectors.

The symptom of the bottom two rungs is **context re-work**: the same background
re-explained every session. It is invisible because each instance is small.

## Foundational skills

### Verification

- **None** — No formal protocol for high-stakes output.
- **Passive scan** — Output is skimmed for obvious problems.
- **Deep logic verification** — Reasoning and logic are checked, not just the prose.

Verification rigour has to rise with agency. Delegating objectives without a
verification protocol is the failure mode that ends AI adoption programmes.

### Leadership scope

- **Purely personal use** — Fluency stops at the individual.
- **Team practice** — Prompts and patterns shared with the team.
- **Redesigning the org** — Roles and workflows actively redesigned around what AI can now do.

## Productivity and strategy

### Decision support

- **Rarely** — AI executes decisions already made.
- **Sometimes** — AI is asked for options on larger decisions.
- **Routinely** — AI is used for option generation, to surface paths not originally considered.

Option generation is the use with the largest gap between value and adoption. Most
people who are fluent everywhere else still bring AI a decision they have already made.

## The three patterns

### The Prompt User — high effort, low systemic leverage

AI is being treated as a conversational search engine. The tell is context re-work:
re-explaining goals every time a chat opens. Engagement is transactional — real
individual task wins, no infrastructure that lets AI act as a persistent extension of
how this person works.

Raw material for the one experiment:
- Move from prompt-only to persistent context using projects or custom instructions.
- Adopt structural prompting to cut iteration cycles.
- Pick one recurring weekly task and automate it.

### The Augmented Lead — strong collaboration, limited scale

Augmentation is mastered; the back-and-forth is genuinely good. But the tool is still
being babysat through steps it could handle independently, and the workflows are not
systemic — high personal output, nothing another person could pick up.

Raw material for the one experiment:
- Take one research workflow and delegate the *how*, not just the task.
- Start writing prompts down as team operating procedures.
- Shift the focus from personal productivity to team change.

### The Workflow Architect — high systemic leverage

Not using AI so much as designing an environment for it. Persistent and living context
make the AI a real extension of the work. The next frontier is diligence — keeping
quality as autonomy rises — and organisational traction: scaling these habits past the
people who already have them.

Raw material for the one experiment:
- Connect living context through MCP or API access to real systems.
- Formalise the decision-support pattern as a standard others can follow.
- Treat AI safety and verification as a leadership constraint, not a compliance task.

## Reading combinations

The dimensions are more useful crossed than averaged:

- **High augmentation, low agency** — the Agency Ceiling. Fix verification, not prompting.
- **High agency, low verification** — the dangerous quadrant. Slow down before scaling.
- **High everything, purely personal use** — the org is getting one person's leverage.
  This is the most common shape among strong individual users in leadership roles, and
  the biggest missed opportunity.
- **Persistent context, low automation** — good infrastructure, unused. Usually means
  nobody has looked at the week for recurring work.
