# Facilitation and Visual Management (single initiative)

This skill sniffs **one initiative**. For board-wide patterns across many initiatives (shadowing, WIP clustering, portfolio risk balance), use the `sniff-test-portfolio` add-on.

## When to run a single-initiative sniff

- **During a decision** — at intake, during Explore, at the Plan/Commit investment decision, or at the Plan/Commit → Execute/Build "point of last return." The sniff directly informs the call.
- **On readiness** — when someone says an initiative is "ready to build," sniff before agreeing.
- **On loop-back** — when a BAU surprise sends an initiative back to Discovery, sniff what actually broke.
- **To build the muscle** — sniff one initiative as a worked example before rolling the practice out.

## Who to involve

- The initiative owner / DRI (owns the canvas and its accuracy).
- A sponsor or decision-maker when the sniff feeds a go/no-go.
- The right cross-functional stakeholders for the open risks (EA for architecture, SecOps for security, data, finance/AOP) — engaged early as stakeholders, not late as gatekeepers.

## Establish "what you can count on" before rating

If the org has a stage-level **executive contract** ("when an initiative is in Discovery, here's what I can rely on being true"), use it as the bar. If not, use the default rubric in `lifecycle-clarity-rubric.md` and say you're using a default. Frame the read in **confidence** terms ("common language, not gates"), not "gates" or "exit criteria."

## Visual management

- **Sniff Test card** — one card capturing the initiative's clarity read across the Lean Product Canvas / DVF dimensions, each tagged evidence-backed or opinion. Print/laminate for in-person, or a Miro/Mural template for virtual.
- **Dots on the initiative's Kanban card** — visualize the open question marks, then **move dots into the green as assumptions are burned down**. The card makes both current risk and derisking progress visible at a glance.

## Single-initiative run-of-show

1. **Frame** (2–3 min): name the initiative, its altitude (Tier-1 or not), the decision this sniff feeds, and the "what you can count on" bar.
2. **Verify the stage**: confirm the claimed stage against evidence; flag any mismatch before rating.
3. **Sniff**: rate each dimension, tag evidence vs. opinion, compare to the stage expectation, mark the card.
4. **Spot smells & watermelons**: name mismatches and confidently-held-but-unevidenced clarity plainly.
5. **Decide derisking moves**: for each flag, name the next conversation or cheapest test and who owns it; note the go/no-go implication.
6. **Close**: capture the card/result where it's visible; agree when to re-sniff (e.g., before the next commitment point).

## Where you'll face resistance (and how to engage it)

- **"This needs a lot of time to be useful."** A *very lightweight* review has high value; keep the first passes short.
- **"Some high-profile initiatives won't bear scrutiny."** That's exactly the risk the sniff exists to surface. Expect sensitivity when a weak case is exposed early; frame it as derisking and evidence-building, not blame.
- **"This is over-engineering up-front thinking."** The goal is *just enough* clarity for the stage, steered by evidence and leading indicators — not big up-front design.
- **The rubber stamp.** If a commitment is already decided and the sniff is expected to ratify it, say so plainly and capture the untested assumptions as the diagnostic record — that record is what shifts behavior over time.

## Challenges to manage

- Balancing **consistency** of the read against **prematurely locking in** criteria.
- Creating time to improve *this* initiative's clarity while yesterday's shortfalls compete for attention.
- Securing support to **act on** the sniff (a sniff that changes nothing is theater).
