# Derisking and Leading Indicators

A sniff test asks not only "do we have enough clarity for the stage?" but "**is our approach to derisking appropriate for the risk level?**" This is the Risk/Derisking Matrix plus the leading-indicator theory that makes sense-and-respond steering possible.

## The Risk/Derisking Matrix — match the approach to the risk

Map the initiative by **risk level** (Low / Medium / High / Potential) against **knowledge** (Desirability / Viability / Feasibility), then pick a proportionate approach. Over-investing in discovery for a low-risk item wastes scarce enterprise capacity; under-investing on a high-risk Tier-1 bet burns money on the wrong thing.

| Risk level | Approach | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | **Just Do It** | High, evidence-backed clarity across D/V/F; cost of being wrong is small. |
| **Medium** | **Ship & Measure** | Plausible conviction but you want a real-world read; you can release a slice and learn from usage. |
| **High** | **Discovery / Testing**, or **Seek Alpha** | Major open question marks (especially Desirability); cost of building the wrong thing is high. Seek Alpha = go after the riskiest unknown deliberately. |

Mantra: **Learn before you Burn (money).** Discovery is timeboxed, pre-commitment risk reduction — its job is to build confidence for a real investment decision, not to elaborate requirements for an already-committed build.

**Smell**: an approach chosen by habit or politics rather than the actual risk profile — heavyweight discovery on a low-risk tweak, or "just build it" on a high-uncertainty strategic bet (the rubber stamp).

## Leading vs. lagging indicators (sense-and-respond)

- **Lagging indicators** are the rear-view mirror — known only after the fact. Important as goals, useless as a steering mechanism under uncertainty.
- **Leading indicators** show where we are *now*. They power the sense-and-respond loop: define them, monitor them, respond. Orient them around the **main sources of uncertainty** (the D/V/F question marks).

Example leading indicators (initiative- and portfolio-level): % of initiatives with a reviewed Lean Product Canvas before Execute/Build; % where major risks surface in Discovery rather than in Rollout / BAU; reduction in "urgent/unplanned" items after committed start; frequency of honest loop-backs from BAU to Discovery; sponsor confidence on predictability.

## The key challenge

A real read on leading indicators usually requires **working product increments** and a **feedback loop**, which forces three hard things:

1. Clarify the **leap-of-faith / riskiest assumptions** about what will drive the outcomes.
2. Identify **small yet useful slices**.
3. Build a **feedback loop that includes working product**, ideally used by intended users.

All three are hard — which is why teams default to easier-but-useless proxies. The sniff test's job is to notice when that's happening and name it.

## The input → impact ladder (trading certainty for responsiveness)

| Level | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Input** | What the org spends money on | budget to test an improvement idea |
| **Activity** | What people do | write code, hold meetings |
| **Output** | What the org produces | releases, reviews |
| **Outcome** | A new/improved capability the user experiences | user can save/earn more than before |
| **Impact** | Results the org/investors achieve when users reach their outcomes | revenue, profit, market share |

- **Impact/Outcome = high certainty, lagging.** You know for sure you achieved the result — but only after the fact.
- **Input/Activity/Output = fast feedback, weak signal.** Quick to read, but what do they really say about progress toward the result?
- **Goldilocks zone**: a leading indicator that gives feedback within a *reasonable time frame* **and** has *reasonable correlation* to the desired result. Aim here. Think **OMTM** (One Metric That Matters) for the current riskiest assumption.

**Smell**: steering by activity/output ("we shipped 12 features," "the team is busy") and calling it progress, with no line of sight to an outcome.
