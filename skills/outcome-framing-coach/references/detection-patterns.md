# Detection Patterns

Use these heuristics when reviewing a draft.

## 1. Prescriptive Language

Flag wording that tells the team what to build, ship, or execute without clarifying the underlying intent.

Common patterns:
- `Build the ...`
- `Launch the ...`
- `Ship the ...`
- `Implement ...`
- `Add ...`
- `Create ...`
- `Finish ...`
- `Make sure you ...`
- `I need you to ...`

Why this is a problem:
- It hard-codes the solution before the team has a chance to think.
- It optimizes for compliance with an output, not learning or impact.
- It often hides the actual problem that needs solving.

## 2. Over-Directive Language

Flag wording that sounds like a command but gives no context, no problem, and no why.

Common patterns:
- `We need to ...`
- `Tell the team to ...`
- `My expectation is ...`
- `Let's make sure ...`
- `I was thinking we should ...`

Why this is a problem:
- These are drive-by directives.
- Teams infer that the leader has already decided the solution.
- It kills initiative and invites low-quality compliance.

## 3. What Not To Flag Automatically

Do not automatically flag:
- Compliance, legal, security, or customer-contract requirements with a clear reason.
- Actual non-negotiables where the why is explicit.
- Requests that already connect action to a real problem and clear impact.

Example:
- `We must complete SOC 2 evidence collection by June 15 to keep the enterprise renewal on track.`

That is still directive, but the why is clear. The coaching move is to tighten autonomy around the approach, not pretend the constraint does not exist.

## 4. Diagnostic Questions

Ask these while reviewing:
- What problem is this sentence trying to solve?
- Is the leader prescribing a solution instead of naming the need?
- Does the team know why this matters now?
- Would a capable team member know what good looks like without being told the exact tactic?
- Is there room left for the team to propose a better path?

## 5. Labels To Use

- `prescriptive output`
- `drive-by directive`
- `missing why`
- `solution-first framing`
- `autonomy-killing wording`

Use blunt labels, but keep them tied to the actual sentence.
